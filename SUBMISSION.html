1. Summary

Voice agents are being deployed across India faster than anyone can test them. Teams QA them with about 10 polite calls in clean English, then ship. The failures that follow are Indian failures: Hinglish the recogniser drops mid sentence, invented discounts, hallucinated content claims, angry customers who never reach a human.

Crucible attacks a voice agent with synthetic Indian customers, in real speech, and returns an evidence pinned report of where it breaks. The customer is built entirely on Sarvam: Sarvam-30B is its brain, Bulbul v3 its voice, Saarika v2.5 measures what the target's recogniser did to it, and Sarvam-105B judges every transcript against a per scenario answer key.

We ran it against a real production retention agent deployed by a major Indian streaming service (name withheld here, shared privately on request). Across 34 live conversations and 446 turns, 7 of them in full voice, Crucible found defects manual QA had missed: 4 rule breaches in one call, a turn where the agent's recogniser kept 1 word out of 28 and the agent answered anyway, and a swing from 82.5 to 15.0 across identical scenarios because the agent is inconsistent between calls.

This document explains the product and, in sections 4 to 6, how it works. The recommendation: judge us on section 3. The report is the product.

2. The problem

An Indian voice agent fails in ways its builders never see, because the test that would catch the failure is never run.

  • Manual QA samples one draw from a distribution. Our data shows the same agent, same scenario, scoring 82.5 in one call and 15.0 in another. About 10 polite calls land on one number and call it the truth.
  • The existing tools cannot speak to Indian agents. Coval, Cekura and Hamming AI simulate American English callers. None can produce "thoda discount de do na yaar" as a bargaining move, so none can test how an agent handles it.
  • The worst failures exist only in audio. The same agent that passes a text test dropped 27 of 28 spoken words in one live turn and responded as if nothing happened. No transcript level evaluation can see that.

The cost lands in production, on retention and collections calls, at the exact moment the customer is most likely to leave.

The control is a validity gate, not a data point. One persona (a cooperative customer) is designed to be easy. If the agent fails the easy one, the harness cannot tell agent failure from its own failure, so the run refuses to accuse the agent of anything.

5. One voice turn, in detail

The voice loop is half duplex: one party speaks at a time, like a phone call. The engineering problem is that the target platform gives no end of turn event, streams background noise forever, and drops the socket if our side goes quiet at the protocol level.

Agent speaks
(audio frames, about 300ms each)
Amplitude detector:
1.5s below the speech floor
= turn over
Sarvam-30B composes the reply,
in character, in Hinglish
Bulbul v3 speaks it
(16 kHz PCM, cast voice)
Stream at real speed,
then hold the mic until the
agent's ASR returns (8s bound)
user_transcript arrives
"What the agent heard"
recorded beside what we said.
Next turn begins.
Prevented failure
Compute blocks the socket,
no pong goes out,
platform drops the call.

3 findings from live measurement shaped this loop. Each replaced an assumption that was wrong.

  • The socket dies from a missing pong, not missing speech. A 7 conversation controlled experiment (2 by 2 plus controls) showed 112s of silence survives if protocol pongs keep flowing, and any arm without them dies. So a permanently live reader owns the socket and model calls never block it.
  • There is no end of turn event, and audio never stops. The platform streams continuous office background noise. We detect end of turn by amplitude: speech peaks near 10% of full scale, the noise floor near 9%, and 1.5 seconds of quiet closes the turn. Calibrated over 8 captured turns, it reproduces every boundary exactly.
  • Streaming silence as a keepalive is harmful. Zero filled audio convinces the agent's recogniser the mic is live. It endpoints empty turns, asks "are you still there", and hangs up at 59s. Between turns we send nothing at all.

If the agent's recogniser never returns a transcript within 8 seconds of our last real audio, the turn ends as a hard error. That state is a bug, never a slow turn, and treating it as one would deadlock the call.

6. How a score earns trust

A judge that cannot prove its claims is a liability, because this product exists to tell teams uncomfortable things about their agent. Every claim therefore passes 3 audits in code before a human ever sees it.

with a quote
Verbatim audit: is the quote
character for character
in that turn
Speaker audit: did the right
party say it (a customer line
cannot convict the agent)
Ground truth audit: which
listed rule does the quote
actually breach
All 3 pass
Finding kept:
quote, turn number, rule
Any audit fails
Finding discarded.
Never shown to the reader.

2 more properties protect the verdict:

  • The judge is effectively deterministic. 27 of 28 dimension scores were identical across 3 independent judge passes on the same transcripts. Score movement between runs is the agent moving, not the judge.
  • Speech derived text can never convict. We measured the recogniser inventing "20%". So a number that arrived through ASR can only flag a finding for review, never prove a violation. A fact is only a fact when the text is verbatim.

Cross call analysis catches what no single call can. Personas carry deliberately distinct discount ceilings (5, 10, 15, 25%), so a number bleeding from one conversation into another is a provable invention. And a failure in 3 of 3 calls is a defect where a failure in 1 is an anecdote.

7. How Sarvam's models drive the product

Sarvam is the product, not an add on. The entire synthetic customer (brain, mouth, ears) and the entire evaluation layer (judge, referee, report) run on Sarvam. The agent under test can come from any vendor; our current target runs ElevenLabs with a Qwen LLM, which also means the judge never grades its own model family.

RoleModelWhat it does
Customer brainSarvam-30BPlays 4 handcrafted personas in Hinglish, Hindi and Indian English. About 5s a turn at temperature 0.9.
Customer voiceBulbul v34 voices cast by measured pitch across the 37 speaker roster (111 to 195 Hz), so a 21 year old and a 45 year old do not sound alike.
Listener fidelitySaarika v2.5Re transcribes our own audio each turn to measure what the target's recogniser dropped or invented.
Judge, referee, reportSarvam-105BScores 7 rubric dimensions, decides soft conversation endings, writes the report narrative. Every quote re verified in code.

Sarvam has no hosted agent product, so the customer is our own loop built from Sarvam building blocks. The models' reasoning behaviour (reasoning cannot be disabled and consumes the token budget first) forced real engineering: measured retry ladders, a 4096 token tier cap discovered live, and one judge call per rubric dimension. The judge's Indic competence is load bearing: language handling carries the second highest rubric weight, and Sarvam-105B is what lets a Devanagari quote match a Devanagari turn.

9. Traction and the path to 25 users

We will not claim users we do not have. Today Crucible has certified 1 production agent and has been used by our own team.

The path to 25 real users starts at Epoch itself and uses the product's own design: it works on agents we did not build.

  1. Certify other builders' agents. Dozens of Epoch teams built voice agents. Each team that submits an endpoint and receives a report is a real user holding a real artifact. Target: 10 to 15 certifications on 30 July.
  2. Persona studio as the self serve door. Upload one recorded call, get a persona, run it against your agent. A 2 minute floor conversation becomes a signup.
  3. Design partners. The streaming service whose agent we certified, and 2 voice agent platforms, are the first paying conversations. Every Indian company deploying retention or collections voice agents runs the same 10 polite calls today.

The report is the growth loop. Every certification hands a team a document containing its own defects, which is the strongest reason to return after every prompt change. Agents change weekly; certification is bought once and used forever.

10. Order of work between now and Epoch

Hosting first, because it is the only checkpoint criterion the product does not already meet.

Phase 1: public deployment (start here)

Dashboard and API on a public URL, serving the recorded runs. Stable and open to anyone.

Builds: hosting, runs bundle, health check.

Phase 2: Epoch certification signup

A form that takes a team's agent endpoint and returns a report the same day. This is the 25 user mechanism.

Builds: signup, adapter for submitted endpoints, report delivery.

Phase 3: Tamil and Telugu personas

2 new personas with cast Bulbul voices, making the language claim visible on stage.

Builds: 2 persona YAML files, voice casting, ground truth keys.

Phase 4: human agreement at n=10

10 conversations scored blind by a human, per dimension agreement computed and published whatever it says.

Builds: blind scoring flow, agreement report.

11. How we will measure success

  • A public URL serving the dashboard, up through Epoch and after it.
  • 25 real users, counted as teams or companies holding a certification report of their own agent.
  • Provable defects delivered per certified agent, with quote and rule, starting from the 4 found in the first certification.
  • Judge and human agreement at n=10, published with the number it lands on.
